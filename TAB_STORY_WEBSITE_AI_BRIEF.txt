TAB STORY — PREMIUM MARKETING WEBSITE BUILD BRIEF

ROLE
You are a senior product designer, UX writer, brand designer, and frontend engineer. Design and build a polished, conversion-focused marketing website for Tab Story. The result must feel like a real premium SaaS product—not a generic template, browser-extension directory page, or AI-generated landing page.

PRODUCT SUMMARY
Tab Story is a modern tab-saving, organization, scheduling, reminder, and reading product available in two connected experiences:

1. Tab Story Browser Extension
   - Saves the current tab or multiple tabs.
   - Organizes saved pages into collections.
   - Supports notes, tags, pinning, history, and review workflows.
   - Schedules saved tabs and delivers reminders.
   - Includes a “Discuss with AI” workflow.
   - Can preserve readable article content for offline reading.
   - Works directly inside the browser through a focused side panel.

2. Tab Story PWA
   - Provides a modern mobile and desktop web experience.
   - Lets users access, organize, schedule, review, and read saved content.
   - Shares the same account and synchronized data with the extension.
   - Provides an installable app-like experience without requiring an app store.

The extension and PWA should be presented as two parts of one connected product. The core value proposition is not merely “save tabs.” Tab Story helps people turn temporary browser tabs into an organized, searchable, scheduled personal knowledge and reading system.

PRIMARY WEBSITE GOALS

1. Explain the product in under five seconds.
2. Make the browser extension the primary acquisition action.
3. Present the PWA as the companion experience for accessing saved content anywhere.
4. Communicate the benefits of scheduling, reminders, collections, offline reading, AI discussion, and cross-device sync.
5. Convert free users into premium users without making the free plan feel useless.
6. Establish trust around privacy, data ownership, reliable reminders, and synchronization.
7. Look exceptional on mobile, tablet, laptop, and large desktop screens.

TARGET USERS

- People who keep too many browser tabs open.
- Researchers and students collecting sources.
- Professionals saving articles, documents, dashboards, and reference pages.
- Readers who want to return to content at the right time.
- Knowledge workers who want notes, tags, collections, AI discussion, and reminders in one workflow.
- Users who switch between desktop and mobile and require synchronized access.

BRAND POSITIONING

Position Tab Story as calm, intelligent, dependable, and premium. It should feel like a blend of a refined productivity tool, a reading app, and a personal knowledge companion.

Suggested positioning statement:
“Turn open tabs into organized plans, reminders, and knowledge.”

Suggested supporting statement:
“Save from your browser, organize without friction, and return to every important page at the right moment—on any device.”

Do not describe Tab Story as another bookmark manager. Emphasize that bookmarks are passive, while Tab Story makes saved content actionable through scheduling, reminders, review, offline access, and AI-assisted understanding.

VOICE AND WRITING STYLE

- Clear, confident, calm, and human.
- Premium without sounding arrogant.
- Short sentences and concrete benefits.
- Avoid inflated claims such as “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” or “the future of productivity.”
- Avoid excessive technical jargon on marketing sections.
- Do not use placeholder copy or lorem ipsum.
- Do not claim security certifications, user counts, ratings, integrations, or performance numbers unless actual data is supplied.

VISUAL DIRECTION

Create a sophisticated dark-first interface inspired by the Tab Story product UI, while remaining original and suitable for a public marketing website.

Recommended visual language:

- Near-black background with layered charcoal surfaces.
- Soft borders and subtle glass effects rather than heavy gradients everywhere.
- Accent colors: electric indigo, violet, emerald/mint, and carefully limited coral/red for urgency.
- Large, crisp typography with strong hierarchy.
- Rounded cards with intentional spacing and restrained shadows.
- Product UI cards should be the visual hero: saved-tab cards, scheduled states, tags, notes, reminders, and AI actions.
- Use tasteful motion: card stacking, scheduling transitions, subtle sync indicators, and scroll reveals.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
- Maintain strong color contrast and keyboard-visible focus states.
- Avoid excessive floating blobs, meaningless 3D objects, stock photography, and generic dashboard illustrations.

The product should visually feel organized and calm even when demonstrating many saved tabs.

SECTION 10 — PRIVACY AND RELIABILITY

“Your saved web deserves dependable storage.”

Communicate:

- Notification access is requested in context, not aggressively at first launch.
- Users should be able to delete their data and account.
- AI-related data handling should be explained separately and precisely.

Do not claim end-to-end encryption unless it is truly implemented. Do not claim that no data reaches the server because server-side synchronization is part of the product.

IMPORTANT PRODUCT BEHAVIOR TO REFLECT

- Free users can save and synchronize tabs without a tab-count paywall.
- Premium access applies to AI Tools such as summarization, key points, explanations, extraction, translation, related topics, and page Q&A.
- The server is authoritative when accepting or rejecting synchronized records.
- Collections delete their children recoverably through synchronized soft deletion.
- The extension and PWA perform push-before-pull synchronization.
- These protocol mechanics should shape trustworthy behavior but should not be exposed as marketing jargon.
